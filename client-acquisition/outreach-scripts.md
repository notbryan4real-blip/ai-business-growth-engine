# First-client outreach scripts

Personalize every message with a real observation. Never claim a business lacks a website until you have checked its name, location, social profiles, and likely domain.

## First contact: ask permission to show a concept

Hi [Owner/Business Name] — I came across [Business Name] while looking at [service] providers in [city]. I noticed [specific, verified observation]. I build simple, mobile-friendly websites that make services and quote requests easy to find. I had an idea for how a page for your business could look. Would you be open to seeing a quick concept? No pressure either way.

## If they say yes

Thanks! I'll put together a concept showing the layout and how customers could find your services and contact you. It would be a draft for discussion, not a live site. If you like the direction, I can quote a fixed-scope one-page website for $500, with the scope and any hosting/domain costs agreed up front.

## If the business already has a website

Thanks for clarifying. I won't assume you need a replacement. If useful, I can point out one specific improvement I noticed and you can decide whether it's worth addressing.

## Follow-up after 3–5 business days

Hi [Name] — just following up once on my note about a possible website concept for [Business Name]. If it's not a priority right now, no worries; I won't keep messaging. Would you like me to send the idea?

## Phone opener

Hi, is this [Business Name]? My name is [Your Name]. I found your business while researching [service] in [city]. I have one specific idea for making your services easier for customers to review and request a quote on mobile. Is the owner or manager the right person to ask about that? If this isn't a good time, I can leave it there.

## Avoid these mistakes

- Don't say "your website is broken" unless you tested it and have evidence.
- Don't claim a missing Google Maps website link proves the company has no website.
- Don't invent revenue losses, traffic numbers, rankings, testimonials, or customer results.
- Don't present a mockup as an official site or imply the business hired you.
- Don't send repeated messages after a clear no. Follow applicable email, texting, and telemarketing rules.
- Don't complete the full project before agreeing in writing on scope and payment.

## Simple close

Before starting, confirm in writing:
- One-page website and the exact sections included.
- $500 total project fee and payment schedule.
- One revision round.
- Who supplies and approves text, images, logo, phone, address, and service details.
- Domain and hosting costs are separate unless explicitly included.
- Target delivery date after the client provides required materials.
- Client owns the approved deliverables after full payment.
