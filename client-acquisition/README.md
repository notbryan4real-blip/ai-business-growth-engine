# First-client kit: $500 small-business website package

This kit is designed to help land the first paid website project using an Android phone and free tools. It is a starting workflow, not a promise of sales.

## Offer to sell

**Mobile-friendly one-page business website — $500 fixed project fee**

Suggested scope:
- One responsive page with business overview, services, service area, trust signals, and contact/quote calls to action.
- One revision round after the client reviews the first draft.
- Basic page title, meta description, semantic headings, and image alt text.
- Help publishing to a hosting account controlled by the client.
- Delivery of the HTML/CSS/source files.

Be explicit about exclusions: domain registration, paid hosting, paid booking/CRM tools, copywriting beyond the agreed page, photography, e-commerce, ongoing SEO, and additional pages are not included unless agreed in writing. Do not promise search rankings or a specific number of leads.

## No-cost workflow

1. Search Google Maps for one trade in one city. Prioritize independent service businesses with a working phone number, signs of recent activity, and meaningful reviews.
2. Verify the website situation yourself. A missing website link is a lead signal, not proof that no site exists. Search the business name and city, check social profiles, and open any domain you find.
3. Pick a business whose visible gap matches the offer. Record only public business contact details and a specific, factual observation.
4. Create a clearly labeled private concept demo. Do not publish it as the business's official site or use its logo/photos without permission.
5. Send one personalized, respectful message or call. Ask whether they would like to see the concept; do not attach a huge pitch.
6. If interested, confirm scope, total price, timeline, revision limit, ownership, hosting costs, and payment milestones in writing. Take an agreed deposit before doing the full build.
7. Deliver only after the agreed payment terms are met. Keep the client's domain and accounts in their control.

## Prospect qualification score (0–10)

- 0–2: active business evidence (recent reviews/photos or current social activity)
- 0–2: a real website gap verified by manual search
- 0–2: clear service and contact information
- 0–2: customer acquisition is plausibly valuable (quotes, bookings, high-value jobs)
- 0–2: a public contact channel and a personalized reason to reach out

Do not score a business highly solely because it has no website. Skip closed, dubious, or inactive listings.

## Suggested free tools

- Google Maps / Search for prospect research.
- GitHub for source files and version history.
- A free static host only if its current terms meet the client's needs.
- A spreadsheet or the CSV in this folder for tracking.
- A payment provider only after reviewing its fees and requirements; don't pay for a subscription just to get started.

## Demo template

Open `demo/index.html` as the starting point for a fictional mobile detailing company. Replace every sample detail with client-approved information before showing it as a tailored concept. Keep it clearly labeled as a concept until the business authorizes publication.

## Success metric

The first milestone is not automation. It is **10 carefully verified prospects, 10 personalized contacts, and at least 2 follow-ups**. Track replies and objections, then improve the offer. Results are uncertain; never claim a sale is guaranteed.
